Punta Gorda, Florida

Building in Punta Gorda

Punta Gorda is small — roughly 19,500 people — but it may be the most varied place we build. On one side of town are Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles, deed-restricted canal communities where nearly every home has a dock and a view down a seawalled waterway toward Charlotte Harbor. On the other is one of Southwest Florida's oldest downtowns, with tin-roofed cottages, brick-paved streets, and a historic district the city actively protects.

A home builder and remodeler working in Punta Gorda needs to be comfortable in both worlds. In PGI and BSI, the conversation is about flood elevation, seawall setbacks, maximizing the water view, and satisfying the architectural review requirements that many sections carry. In the historic district near Gilchrist Park and Retta Esplanade, it is about respecting original proportions and materials while quietly bringing wiring, plumbing, and wind resistance up to modern standards.

Brunderman Building Company has been part of this community for four decades. Our office address is in Punta Gorda, and after Hurricane Charley made landfall here in 2004 the rebuilding effort reshaped much of the city — including the way homes are now engineered. We carry those lessons into every project.

Local Knowledge

Waterfront and Historic Homes Need Different Expertise

Waterfront lots in Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles sit in FEMA flood zones, and new homes must be built with the finished floor at or above the required elevation. That frequently means a stem-wall foundation with compacted fill, carefully planned driveway slopes, and garage floors set lower than living areas where allowed. For remodels of older ground-level PGI homes, FEMA's 50% substantial-improvement rule is a central planning issue: exceed the threshold and the entire home must be elevated or otherwise brought into compliance. We help owners scope and phase projects with that limit clearly in view.

In the downtown historic district, exterior changes are subject to city review, and the right approach is usually restoration-minded: repairing rather than replacing where possible, matching siding profiles and window proportions, and keeping additions subordinate to the original structure. Inland neighborhoods such as Burnt Store Meadows and Deep Creek are more conventional, and well suited to straightforward custom homes and remodeling.

FAQ

Punta Gorda Building & Remodeling FAQ

Do you build in Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles?

Yes. We build new waterfront homes and remodel existing ones in both communities, and we work within the deed restrictions and architectural review requirements that apply to many sections.

My PGI home is at ground level. How much remodeling can I do?

If the home is below the current required flood elevation, improvements are limited by FEMA's 50% rule as administered by the City of Punta Gorda. The limit is based on the value of the structure, not the land. We help you understand the numbers before designing the project.

Can you work on a home in the historic district?

We can. Exterior work in the district typically requires city review, and we plan projects to be compatible with the home's original character while improving its structure, systems, and storm resistance.
