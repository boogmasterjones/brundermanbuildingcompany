## Why Windows and Doors Matter in Hurricane Hardening

A house is only as strong as its weakest opening. 

That is why openings are a core part of hurricane hardening. On a new home, Brunderman Building Co Inc pairs impact-rated windows and doors with concrete block construction and a continuous load path, so the roof, walls and foundation work together. On an older home, replacing the openings is one of the most practical ways to improve how the house holds up in a storm.

## What Drives the Cost

Every home is different. These are the factors that move the number the most:

### Number and size of openings

This is the biggest factor. A home with a few standard windows is a different job from a home with many openings, tall windows, transoms or large expanses of glass. Larger openings add to the scope of the job.

### Sliding glass doors

Impact-rated sliders are substantial units, and multi-panel or oversized sliders add to the cost. They often need careful work at the threshold to manage water.

### Entry doors and garage-side doors

Front entry doors, French doors and side doors all count as openings. Decorative glass, sidelights and custom sizes add to the scope.

### Stucco, trim and interior finish repair

After the new unit is installed, the exterior needs to be patched, sealed and painted, and interior trim, drywall returns or sills may need repair. Homes with older damage, previous patchwork or rot around the frames can need more repair work than expected once the old units come out.

### Opening changes

Keeping each opening the same size is simpler. Enlarging a window, adding a door or closing one in means structural work, which adds time and cost.

### Permits and inspections

The paperwork and inspection steps are part of the job and part of the cost.

## Combining Window Replacement With a Remodel

Windows and doors are often best done as part of a larger project. If you are already planning a kitchen remodel, an open floor plan conversion, a lanai enclosure or an addition, it can make sense to replace the openings at the same time. Walls are already being opened, stucco and paint are already on the schedule, and the new layout may call for a different window or door size anyway.

## Questions to Ask Before You Start

- Which openings are the priority if the whole house cannot be done at once?
- Are any openings changing size or location?
- What condition are the walls and frames in around the existing units?
- Is other work planned in the next few years that could be done at the same time?
