Port Charlotte, Punta Gorda, North Port, Venice, and the rest of our service area sit inside the Florida Building Code's wind-borne debris region. In practical terms, that means every exterior opening on a new home, and every window or door you replace on an existing one, must either be impact-rated or be protected by an approved shutter system. The code gives you the choice. After four decades of building here, we have a clear preference.

## How Each One Works

**Impact-rated windows and doors** use laminated glass, a tough plastic interlayer bonded between two panes, set in a reinforced frame and anchored to the structure. Tested products are hit with a 9-pound 2×4 fired from a cannon, then cycled through thousands of positive and negative pressure changes. The glass may crack, but it stays in the frame, and the building stays sealed.

**Hurricane shutters** protect ordinary glass by covering it. Options range from inexpensive steel or aluminum panels that bolt on before a storm, to accordion and roll-down shutters that stay mounted year-round, to fabric screens. All approved products pass the same impact tests; the difference is that they only protect the house when someone deploys them.

## Where Shutters Fall Short

-   They have to be put up. Panel shutters mean a ladder, a drill, and hours of work in the days before landfall, often for an older homeowner or a seasonal resident who is not even in Florida.
-   A home with shutters up is dark and hard to leave. A home with impact glass is a normal house during the storm.
-   Panels need storage and get lost or damaged. Roll-down and accordion units add cost, maintenance, and something to look at on every window.
-   Ordinary glass behind a shutter gives none of the everyday benefits below.

## What Impact-Rated Windows Give You Every Day

-   **Noise.** Laminated glass noticeably cuts road, lawn, and neighbor noise.
-   **Energy.** Most impact units are low-E, insulated glass, so the house holds its cool air better than the single-pane aluminum windows in a 1980s home.
-   **UV.** The interlayer blocks most ultraviolet light, which slows fading of floors and furniture.
-   **Security.** The same glass that stops a 2×4 stops a burglar's pry bar.
-   **Insurance.** Opening protection is one of the features Florida wind-mitigation inspections credit, and impact glass qualifies without any action on your part each season.

## Cost and Trade-Offs

Impact-rated windows cost more up front than standard windows plus panel shutters. The gap narrows against accordion or roll-down shutters and disappears when you count energy savings, insurance credits, and the labor of shuttering a house year after year. On a new custom home, the incremental cost of specifying impact glass from the start is modest relative to the total, which is why it is standard on every home we build.

One honest caveat: impact glass is not a substitute for the rest of the structure. A hurricane-proof home is a system: concrete block or properly strapped walls, a roof tied to the foundation with a continuous load path, a wind-rated garage door, and a floor above the flood line. Protected openings keep the pressure out so that system can do its job.

## Our Recommendation

For new construction: impact-rated windows and doors on every opening, no exceptions. For an existing home, replacing the windows with impact-rated units is the single most valuable storm upgrade available, and the one we schedule first on a [whole-home remodel](/services/home-remodeling). If the budget only allows some openings now, start with the large sliders and the windows facing the most exposed direction, and protect the rest with shutters until they can be replaced.
