      <span class="eyebrow">Local Knowledge</span>
      <h2>Waterfront and Historic Homes Need Different Expertise</h2>
      <p>Waterfront lots in Punta Gorda Isles and Burnt Store Isles sit in FEMA flood zones, and new homes must be built with the finished floor at or above the required elevation. That frequently means a stem-wall foundation with compacted fill, carefully planned driveway slopes, and garage floors set lower than living areas where allowed. For remodels of older ground-level PGI homes, FEMA's 50% substantial-improvement rule is a central planning issue: exceed the threshold and the entire home must be elevated or otherwise brought into compliance. We help owners scope and phase projects with that limit clearly in view.</p>
      <p>In the downtown historic district, exterior changes are subject to city review, and the right approach is usually restoration-minded: repairing rather than replacing where possible, matching siding profiles and window proportions, and keeping additions subordinate to the original structure. Inland neighborhoods such as Burnt Store Meadows and Deep Creek are more conventional, and well suited to straightforward custom homes and remodeling.</p>
