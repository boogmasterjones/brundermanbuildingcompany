      <span class="eyebrow">Local Knowledge</span>
      <h2>What to Know Before Building in North Port</h2>
      <p>Most undeveloped North Port lots rely on a private well and a septic system, because city water and sewer reach only part of the platted area. The septic system type is determined by soil and water-table conditions, and the required drain field can take up a meaningful part of the lot — which affects where the house, driveway, and any future pool can go. Lots also need to be checked for protected species: gopher tortoise burrows and scrub-jay habitat both occur here and must be addressed through the proper process before clearing.</p>
      <p>None of this is a reason not to build; it simply needs to be budgeted and scheduled. We walk North Port owners through lot clearing, fill, well and septic, culvert and driveway requirements, and city impact fees up front, so the price of the home reflects the real cost of building on that particular piece of ground. For established neighborhoods like Heron Creek, Bobcat Trail, and Sabal Trace, the conversation shifts to remodeling homes from the 1990s and 2000s that are due for new kitchens, baths, and flooring.</p>
